I've played every position on this team

Ten years ago I was the one staring at a blinking cursor, no roadmap, no idea if any of it would work. Since then I've built products from nothing, run engineering teams, and sat in the CTO chair at a company that grew faster than any plan could keep up with. I've been the founder and the hire, the builder and the one calling the shots — which means I've made most of the mistakes already, so you don't have to.

Outside of work, I shoot film. There's no undo button, no instant preview — just patience, a good eye, and the willingness to get it wrong a few times before you get it right. It turns out that's most of what good engineering is too. The best products, like the best photographs, come from paying close attention and being unafraid to wait for the shot.

01.

The user, always.

Nobody wants your architecture diagram. They want the thing that solves their problem. I've watched brilliant engineers fall in love with their own cleverness and lose the plot entirely — the person on the other end of the product doesn't care how elegant the system is, only whether it works for them.

02.

Fun is a signal.

Fun is not a perk, it's a warning light. If the team building the thing has stopped enjoying the work, the thing they're building is usually already going sideways. I've never seen a miserable team ship something great, and I don't believe I ever will.

03.

Restraint is the work.

The best code is the code you didn't write. Every feature is a future liability. Every line is something someone has to maintain, explain, or eventually rip out. Restraint isn't the absence of ambition — it's usually where the real ambition is hiding.

I'm not interested in being a voice on a monthly call. I want to be in it with you — asking the annoying questions early, building the thing that actually works, and sticking around long enough to see it through. If you're standing where I was a decade ago, idea in hand and nothing else, let's talk.
